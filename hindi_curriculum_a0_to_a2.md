# Hindi Language Curriculum (A0 to B1 Master Framework)

> ### 🎯 Curriculum Vision & Hinglish Learning Philosophy
> * **Target Audience:** Native English speaker and polyglot learning modern **conversational Hinglish** for genuine, spontaneous connection and daily communication with friends and peers (such as Shivani and the South Asian bilingual diaspora).
> * **The Hinglish First Principle:** This curriculum is intentionally designed to avoid lexical burnout. You are **encouraged to use English loanwords** for hyper-specific nouns (e.g., specific fruits, body parts, medical items, transit logistics, technical tools) and adjectives without hesitation. In native urban South Asian speech, Hinglish is the natural prestige register—speaking 100% textbook Sanskritized Hindi in a casual setting sounds artificially formal and stiff.
> * **The Core Structural Priority:** What *cannot* be borrowed from English is the grammatical skeleton: postpositions, pronoun transformations, verb conjugations, compound aspectual auxiliaries (*-lenaa, -denaa, -jaanaa*), the ergative case (*ne*), correlatives, and modal moods.
> * **High-Frequency Vocabulary Anchors:** While specific nouns are plug-and-play in English, certain fundamental words are almost *always* spoken in Hindi even by fluent Hinglish speakers (e.g., *garam/thandaa* for hot/cold, *badaa/chhotaa* for big/small, *saaf/gandaa* for clean/dirty, *rakhnaa/chhodnaa* for keep/leave). To ensure genuine B1 conversational fluency, every third lesson provides a curated high-yield vocabulary anchor that targets these indispensable everyday words while strictly skipping low-yield vocabulary clutter.

## 🟢 Level A0: Absolute Beginner (Foundations & Core Mechanics)

### Lesson A0-X: Pronunciation & Hindi Phonetics
- Dental vs. Retroflex distinctions (Dantya vs. Murdhanya: T/D placement behind teeth vs. curled back on roof)
- Unaspirated vs. Aspirated consonant pairs (air burst mechanics: K vs. Kh, P vs. Ph, T vs. Th)
- Flaps & Retroflex Flaps (Spanish-style tapped *R* vs. the unique Hindi flick *Ṛ / Ṛh*)
- Perso-Arabic loan sounds (*Z, F, Kh, Gh, Q*) & Nasalization (*Anusvara / Chandrabindu*)
- **Phonetics Baseline Note:** All Anki flashcards across the entire deck have pronunciation diacritics included on the second line (macrons for long vowels, under-dots for retroflexes). Dedicated Anki practice cards are not required for this lesson.

### Lesson A0-01: Pronouns, "To Be" Verbs & Hinglish Foundations
- Subject Pronouns (*Main, Tum, Aap, Hum, Vo, Ye*)
- Honorific levels in address (Casual *Tum* vs. Respectful *Aap*)
- Present tense of "To Be" (*Hoon, Hai, Ho, Hain*)
- Speaker Gender agreement: Male verb endings (-taa, -aa, -ūngaa)
- Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) sentence order (Tamil alignment)
- Natural Hinglish greetings and present state (*"Namaste"*, *"Aap kaise hain?"*, *"Main ready hoon"*)

### Lesson A0-02: Present Continuous & Present Habitual (Doing vs. Does)
- Present Continuous construction (*Verb stem + rahaa/rahe hoon/hai/ho/hain*)
- Expressing ongoing actions (*"Main kaam kar rahaa hoon"*, *"Movie dekh rahaa hoon"*)
- Present Habitual construction (*Verb stem + taa/te hoon/hai/ho/hain*)
- Daily routines, habits, and frequency (*"Main daily gym jaataa hoon"*, *"Toronto mein rahtaa hoon"*)
- Inquiring about routines (*"Tum kyaa kar rahe ho?"*, *"Aap kyaa karte hain?"*)

### Lesson A0-03: Basic Connectors & Postpositions
- Core postpositions (*Mein* - in/at, *Par* - on/at, *Se* - from/with/by)
- Relational compound postpositions (*Ke saath* - with, *Ke liye* - for, *Ke paas* - near/to have, *Ke baare mein* - about)
- Essential logic connectors (*Lekin / Par* - but, *Isliye* - that's why/so, *Kyunki* - because, *Vaise* - by the way)
- Comparative mapping of Hindi postpositions to English and Tamil case markers (*il, aal, udan*)

### Lesson A0-04: Can and Want (Needs, Desires, Likes & Abilities)
- Expressing desires using *Want to* (*Verb-naa + chaahtaa hoon*)
- Expressing necessity/need using *Chaahiye* (*Mujhe [Item] chaahiye*)
- Expressing physical ability/capacity using *Saktaa* (*Verb stem + saktaa hoon*)
- Expressing likes and preferences using *Pasand hai* (*"Mujhe ye place pasand hai"*)
- Stating lack of knowledge (*"Mujhe nahin pataa"*)
- Polite offerings and requests (*"Kyaa aap meri help kar sakte hain?"*)

### Lesson A0-05: The Oblique Shift & Core Postpositions
- Trigger mechanism: Any postposition modifying a preceding noun, pronoun, or verb
- Infinitive verb shift (*-naa* $\rightarrow$ *-ne* before postpositions, e.g., *milnaa* $\rightarrow$ *Shivani se milne ke liye*)
- High-yield action frames (*Verb-ne + ke liye*, *Verb-ne + se pehle*, *Verb-ne + ke baad*)
- Masculine singular noun shift (*-aa* $\rightarrow$ *-e*, e.g., *kamraa* $\rightarrow$ *kamre mein*, *rastaa* $\rightarrow$ *raste par*)
- Demonstrative & pronoun shifts (*Ye* $\rightarrow$ *Is*, *Vo* $\rightarrow$ *Us*, *Kaun* $\rightarrow$ *Kis*)
- Reflexive possessive shift (*Apnaa* $\rightarrow$ *Apne*)

### Lesson A0-06: Simple Past Tense & Completed Actions
- Past tense of "To Be" (*Thaa, Thee, The, Theen*)
- High-frequency irregular completed verbs (*Gayaa/Gaye*, *Aayaa/Aaye*, *Kiyaa*, *Bolaa*)
- Narrating daily past occurrences (*"Kal busy din thaa"*, *"Main kaam par gayaa"*)
- Inquiring about past events (*"Kyaa huaa?"*, *"Aap kahaan gaye the?"*)
- Expressing comprehension (*"Samajh gayaa"*, *"Main samajh gayaa"*)

### Lesson A0-07: Gender Suffixes & Agreement Rules
- Universal rule of thumb for endings:
  - `-aa`: Masculine singular (Main [male], Vo [he], Ye [this boy])
  - `-e`: Masculine plural, formal/respectful address (*Aap, Tum, Hum, Vo/Ye*)
  - `-ii`: Feminine singular & plural (*Main [female], Vo [she], Hum [fem]*)
- Adjective-Noun-Verb harmony across gender and number

### Lesson A0-08: Simple Future Tense & Expressing Plans
- Verb conjugations for future tense (*-ūngaa*, *-oge*, *-enge*)
- Scheduling, promises, and future contact (*"Kal aapko call karūngaa"*, *"Hum jaldi milenge"*)
- Inquiring about upcoming plans (*"Weekend par kyaa karoge?"*)
- Time expressions and durations (*"10 minutes mein pahunchūngaa"*, *"Thodaa time lagegaa"*)

## 🟡 Level A1: Elementary Spoken Hindi (Expanding Everyday Fluency)

### Lesson A1-01: Imperatives & Giving Directions
- Casual vs. Polite Imperatives (*-o* vs. *-iye*: *Karo / Kijiye*, *Aao / Aaiye*, *Dekho / Dekhiye*)
- Negative imperatives (*Mat* for strong prohibitive vs. *Na* for gentle suggestion)
- Directional vocabulary (*Aage, Peeche, Daayein, Baayein, Seedha*)
- Spatial postpositions (*Ke saamne, Ke andar, Ke baahar, Ke paas*)
- Navigating streets, cabs, and transit directions

### Lesson A1-02: Possessives, Relationships & Family
- Possessive pronouns (*Meraa/Merii/Mere*, *Aapkaa/Aapkii/Aapke*, *Iskaa/Uskaa*)
- Possessive postposition *Kaa / Ke / Kee* and its dynamic agreement with the *following* noun
- Describing friends, colleagues, and family (*Dost, Bhai, Behen*)
- Stating background, origin, and age (*"Meri age [number] saal hai"*)

### Lesson A1-03: Multiple Uses of "Ki" vs. "Kaa/Ke/Kee"
- The associative/possessive role of *Kaa/Ke/Kee* (Tamil *udaiya*, French/Spanish *de*)
- The subordinating conjunction *Ki* (Tamil *endru*, French *que*, Spanish *que*, English *that*)
- Linking speech, thoughts, and clauses (*"Maine sochaa ki..."*, *"Mujhe lagtaa hai ki..."*)

### Lesson A1-04: Obligation, Compulsion & Permission
- Habitual / external obligation using *Padtaa hai* (*"Mujhe daily office jaanaa padtaa hai"*)
- Strong personal necessity using infinitive + *Hai / Padegaa* (*"Mujhe nikalnaa hai"*, *"Kaam karnaa padegaa"*)
- Requesting and granting permission (*"Kyaa main andar aa saktaa hoon?"*)
- Rules and prohibitions (*"Yahaan smoking manaa hai"*)

### Lesson A1-05: Habitual Past Tense & Past Continuous Tense
- Habitual Past ("used to do" / past routines): *Verb root + taa/tee/te + thaa/thee/the*
- Past Continuous ("was doing" / past ongoing): *Verb root + rahaa/rahee/rahe + thaa/thee/the*
- Reminiscing about university days, improv memories, and past lifestyle habits

### Lesson A1-06: Communication Verbs (Bolna, Kehna, Bataana, Baat Karna)
- *Bolna*: To speak / produce words (language ability, one-way speech)
- *Kehna*: To say / state a quote or thought (often with *ki*)
- *Bataana*: To tell / inform / provide details to someone (*ko bataana*)
- *Baat karna*: To talk / have a two-way conversation (*se baat karna*)

### Lesson A1-07: Quantifiers, Numbers, Shopping & Bargaining
- Cardinal numbers overview (focus on 1–30 and practical tens: 40, 50, 100)
- Quantifiers (*Bahut, Zyaadaa, Kam, Kuch, Sab, Thodaa*)
- Demonstratives in shopping (*"Ye vaalaa"*, *"Vo vaalaa"*)
- Inquiring prices (*"Ye kitne kaa hai?"*, *"Total kitnaa huaa?"*)
- Bargaining and requesting discounts (*"Thodaa kam karo"*, *"Thodaa mehengaa hai"*)

### Lesson A1-08: Transitive Verbs & Ergative Case Marker (Ne) 🎯 (Current Active Lesson)
- Intransitive verbs (no *ne*, verb agrees with subject) vs. Transitive verbs (requires *ne* in completed past)
- Pronoun transformations with *Ne*: *Maine, Humne, Aapne, Tumne, Usne, Unhone, Kisne*
- Dynamic verb agreement with the object:
  - Masculine singular object: *Maine seb khaayaa* (I ate an apple)
  - Feminine singular object: *Maine chaay pee* (I drank tea)
  - Plural objects: *Maine gaane sune* (I listened to songs)
- Default masculine singular (-aa) when object has *ko* (*Maine Shivani ko dekhaa*) or no explicit object
- Irregular past forms of common transitive verbs (*Karnaa -> kiyaa*, *Lenaa -> liyaa*, *Denaa -> diyaa*, *Peenaa -> peeyaa*)
- Core exceptions that never take *ne*: *Bolnaa* (spoke), *Laanaa* (brought), *Bhoolnaa* (forgot), *Samajhnaa* (understood)

### Lesson A1-09: Conversational Connectors, Discourse Markers & Improv Fillers
- Conversational fillers & discourse glue: *Matlab, Vaise, Yaani, Sach mein?, Arre yaar, Achhaa, Theek hai, Sahi baat hai*
- Chronological sequencers & story transitions: *Pehle, Phir / Uske baad, Aakhiri mein, Iske alaavaa, Varna*
- Reactive conversational tactics: validating, teasing, asking for clarification, and improv callback formulas

### Lesson A1-10: Expressing Opinions, Impressions & Mental States
- Opinion framing formulas: *"Mujhe lagtaa hai ki..."*, *"Mere khayaal se..."*, *"Meraa maannaa hai ki..."*
- Nuances of certainty and doubt: *Shaayad, Ho saktaa hai, Pakkaa nahin, Zaroor*
- Polite agreement & disagreement in Hinglish: *"Sahi baat hai"*, *"Mujhe aisaa nahin lagtaa"*, *"Yeh point sahi hai"*

## 🔴 Level A2: Pre-Intermediate Spoken Hindi (Complex Grammar & Conversational Nuance)

### Lesson A2-01: Compound Verbs & Aspectual Nuances
- Structure: *Main Verb Stem + Explanatory Auxiliary (Lenaa, Denaa, Jaanaa, Daalnaa)*
- Completeness and orientation of action:
  - *-Lenaa*: Action done for self-benefit or inward (*"Khaa lo"*, *"Samajh liyaa"*, *"Soch lo"*)
  - *-Denaa*: Action directed outward for others (*"De diyaa"*, *"Bataa do"*, *"Samjhaa diyaa"*)
  - *-Jaanaa*: Completion, permanence, change of state (*"Ho gayaa"*, *"Chalaa gayaa"*, *"Aa gayaa"*)
  - *-Daalnaa*: Sudden, decisive, or forceful completion (*"Kar daalaa"*, *"Bol daalaa"*)

### Lesson A2-02: Complex Sentence Structures & Correlatives
- Correlative pronoun pairs:
  - *Jo ... Vo* (The one who / that which ... that)
  - *Jahaan ... Vahaan* (Where ... there)
  - *Jab ... Tab* (When ... then)
  - *Jitnaa ... Utnaa* (As much as ... that much)
  - *Jaise ... Vaise* (As / The way ... so / that way)
- Conditional and subordinating conjunctions: *Agar ... toh*, *Kyunki*, *Halaanki ... phir bhi*

### Lesson A2-03: Core Descriptive Adjectives, Sensory Qualities & Polar Opposites 🏷️ (Vocabulary Anchor 1)
- Physical & Temperature Qualities: *Garam* (hot) vs. *Thandaa* (cold), *Meethaa* (sweet) vs. *Teekhaa* (spicy)
- Size & Dimension: *Badaa* (big) vs. *Chhotaa* (small), *Bhaari* (heavy) vs. *Halkaa* (light)
- Speed & Ease: *Tez* (fast/sharp) vs. *Dheere* (slow), *Aasaan* (easy) vs. *Mushkil* (difficult)
- Condition & State: *Saaf* (clean) vs. *Gandaa* (dirty), *Mehengaa* (expensive) vs. *Sastaa* (cheap)
- Truth & Direction: *Sachaa* (true) vs. *Jhoothaa* (false), *Seedhaa* (straight/simple) vs. *Ultaa* (inverted/opposite)

### Lesson A2-04: Dative Experiencer Frameworks (Lagnaa, Aanaa, Pataa, Yaad)
- Experiencer subject with *Ko / Mujhe*:
  - Sensations & states: *Bhookh lagnaa, Pyaas lagnaa, Thand/Garmi lagnaa, Buraa lagnaa, Achhaa lagnaa*
  - Skills and capacities (*Aanaa*): *"Mujhe guitar bajanaa aataa hai"*, *"Mujhe French aatii hai"*
  - Knowledge and facts (*Pataa hona*): *"Mujhe pataa hai ki..."*, *"Kyaa aapko pataa hai?"*
  - Remembering and missing (*Yaad aanaa / Yaad hona*): *"Mujhe university ke din yaad aate hain"*

### Lesson A2-05: The Hinglish Code-Switching Architecture & English Backup Strategies
- The Anatomy of Spoken Hinglish: Safe loanword classes (Nouns, descriptive adjectives) vs. Non-negotiable Hindi grammar (Postpositions, pronouns, obliques, tense auxiliaries)
- The Conjunct Verb Engine: English word + *Karnaa* (active) vs. *Honaa* (passive) vs. *Lagnaa/Feel honaa* (sensory) (*decide karnaa, cancel honaa, weird lagnaa*)
- Aspectual Compound Verbs on English Stems: *"Call kar do"*, *"Check kar lo"*, *"Message send kar diyaa"*, *"Plan cancel ho gayaa"*
- Natural loanword gender assignment rules in spoken Hindi (Why *coffee/meeting* are feminine while *phone/project* are masculine)
- Avoiding false Anglicized calques (Literal word-for-word idioms to avoid)

### Lesson A2-06: High-Yield Spoken Action Verbs & Practical Movement 🏷️ (Vocabulary Anchor 2)
- Handling Objects: *Rakhnaa* (to put/keep), *Uthaanaa* (to pick up/lift), *Chhodnaa* (to leave/drop off), *Pakadnaa* (to catch/hold)
- Movement & Transit: *Pahunchnaa* (to arrive/reach), *Ruknaa* (to pause/stay) vs. *Roknaa* (to stop something), *Chalaanaa* (to drive/operate/run)
- Practical Daily Actions: *Dhoondhnaa* (to search/find), *Milnaa* (to meet/be found), *Girnaa* (to fall), *Bachnaa* (to survive/be saved) vs. *Bachaanaa* (to save)
- Managing & Holding: *Sambhaalnaa* (to manage/handle/take care of)

### Lesson A2-07: The Subjunctive Mood, Permissions & Hypothetical Situations
- The Subjunctive / Optative:
  - Suggestive questions & polite offers: *"Main bolūn?"*, *"Kyaa hum chalein?"*, *"Main order karūn?"*
  - Permission and collaborative decisions: *"Aap baithein"*, *"Vo bole"*
- Counterfactuals and hypotheticals:
  - Regret & wishes: *"Kaash aisaa hotaa!"* (I wish it were so!)
  - Unreal past / conditional imagination: *"Agar main Norwich mein hotaa, toh hum pub jaate..."*

### Lesson A2-08: Core Emotional Descriptors, Mental States & Relational Nuances 🏷️ (Vocabulary Anchor 3)
- Emotional & Psychological States: *Pareshaan* (worried/troubled), *Hairaan* (surprised/shocked), *Shaant* (calm/quiet), *Gussa* (angry), *Khush* (happy) vs. *Udaas* (sad)
- Situational Descriptors: *Ajeeb* (weird/strange), *Zaroori* (important/necessary), *Khaas* (special)
- High-Frequency Discourse Adverbs: *Aamtaur par* (usually/generally), *Achaanak* (suddenly), *Bilkul* (absolutely/completely), *Khaaskar* (especially), *Shaayad* (perhaps)

## 🔵 Level B1: Intermediate Conversational Spoken Hindi (Structural Nuance & Fluid Discourse)

### Lesson B1-01: The Passive Voice & Impersonal Constructions
- Formation: *Verb Stem / Completed Past Participle + Jaanaa* (Inflected in any tense)
- Everyday applications:
  - Completed past passive: *"Email bhej diyaa gayaa"* (The email was sent), *"Meeting cancel kii gayii"* (The meeting was cancelled)
  - Present habitual / general rule passive: *"Aisaa kiyaa jaataa hai"* (It is done like this), *"Yahaan ticket nahin miltaa"*
- Impersonal ability and physical incapacity (*Subject + se + Verb stem + nahin jaataa*):
  - *"Mujhse itnaa wait nahin kiyaa jaataa"* (I can't bear waiting this long / It cannot be waited by me)

### Lesson B1-02: Causative Verbs & Secondary Agency (Karnaa → Karaanaa → Karvaanaa)
- Mechanism of causation: Direct action vs. First causative (causing someone to act) vs. Second causative (having work done through an intermediary)
- High-frequency causative triples:
  - *Karnaa* (to do) $\rightarrow$ *Karaanaa* $\rightarrow$ *Karvaanaa* (*"Maine laptop repair karvaayaa"*)
  - *Khaanaa* (to eat) $\rightarrow$ *Khilaanaa* (to feed/treat someone)
  - *Peenaa* (to drink) $\rightarrow$ *Pilaanaa* (to serve drinks / buy a round)
  - *Dekhnaa* (to see) $\rightarrow$ *Dikhanaaa* (to show)
  - *Sunnaa* (to hear) $\rightarrow$ *Sunaanaa* (to recite / tell a story / play music)
  - *Milnaa* (to meet) $\rightarrow$ *Milaanaa* (to introduce people / connect)
- Intermediary postposition *Se*: *"Maine Shivani se baat karvaayii"* (I had someone speak to Shivani)

### Lesson B1-03: High-Yield Abstract Concepts, Trust & Relational Vocabulary 🏷️ (Vocabulary Anchor 4)
- Trust & Belief: *Bharosaa / Vishwaas* (trust/faith), *Umeed* (hope/expectation), *Sachchaai* (truth)
- Agency & Decisions: *Faislaa* (decision), *Zimmedaari* (responsibility), *Koshish* (effort/attempt), *Haq* (right/entitlement)
- Social Bonds: *Rishtaa* (relationship/connection), *Dosti* (friendship), *Aadat* (habit), *Ehsaas* (realization/feeling)
- Outcomes & Value: *Faaydaa* (benefit/advantage) vs. *Nuqsaan* (loss/harm), *Farq* (difference — *"Isse koi farq nahin padtaa"*)

### Lesson B1-04: Adverbial Participles & Complex Clause Modifiers
- Imperfective Participles (*Verb stem + te hue* — Simultaneous ongoing action):
  - *"Hum coffee peete hue baat karenge"* (We will talk while drinking coffee)
  - *"Chalte-chalte maine sochaa..."* (While walking, I thought...)
- Perfective Participles (*Verb stem + kar / karke* — Sequential completed action / "Having done X"):
  - *"Khaanaa khaakar nikalte hain"* (Having eaten / After eating, let's head out)
  - *"Soch-samajhkar bolo"* (Think carefully before speaking)
- Immediate Sequence (*Verb stem + te hi* — "As soon as"):
  - *"Pahunchte hi mujhe call karnaa"* (Call me as soon as you arrive)

### Lesson B1-05: Conversational Idioms, Expressive Phrasal Formulas & Colloquial Discourse 🏷️ (Vocabulary Anchor 5)
- Street-Smart Conversational Inquiries: *"Chakkar kyaa hai?"* (What's the catch / deal?), *"Kise pataa?"* (Who knows?)
- Intentionality & Nuance: *Jaan-bujhkar* (deliberately/on purpose), *Galti se* (by mistake), *Aise hi* (just like that / casually)
- Conversational Pivot Phrases: *Khair* (anyway / regardless), *Darasal* (as a matter of fact / actually), *Dekhaa jaaye toh* (if you look at it)
- Expressive Hindi Idiomatic Metaphors used in Hinglish:
  - *Dimag kharaab karnaa* (to drive someone crazy / annoy)
  - *Haath dhonaa* (to miss out / lose out on something)
  - *Hawaa nikalnaa* (to lose steam / run out of energy)
  - *Taang kheenchnaa* (to pull someone's leg / tease)

### Lesson B1-06: Presumptive Mood, Deductions & Speculative Future (Hogaa / Kar Rahaa Hogaa)
- Expressing deductions and probability using *Hogaa*:
  - Present state deduction: *"Vo abhi office mein hogaa"* (He must be in the office right now)
  - Present continuous deduction: *"Shivani abhi train mein travel kar rahii hogii"* (Shivani must be traveling on the train right now)
  - Past completed deduction: *"Usne message dekh liyaa hogaa"* (He/She must have seen the message by now)
- Contrasting certainty (*Hai/Thaa*), probability (*Hogaa*), and subjunctive possibility (*Ho / Shaayad*)

### Lesson B1-07: Comprehensive B1 Synthesis & Spontaneous Conversational Fluency
- Complete integration of Passive voice, Causatives, Participles, and Presumptive mood
- Fluid long-form discussions on professional pivots, lifestyle changes, and deep friendship catch-ups
- Natural, uninterrupted code-switching without hesitations or grammar collapses

## 💎 The High-ROI B1 Hinglish Lexicon (Core 200 Anchor List)

> ### 🧠 Why This Lexical Distribution Works for Hinglish (The Pareto Rule)
> In natural urban Hinglish, bilingual speakers **do not replace all words equally**:
> 1. **Verbs (~50 Native Anchors):** English verbs cannot be directly inflected in Hindi without grammatical scaffolding. You either use native Hindi verbs (*karnaa, honaa, lenaa, denaa, aanaa, jaanaa, rakhnaa, etc.*) or apply the **Conjunct Verb Engine** (*[English Noun/Verb] + karnaa/honaa*, e.g., *decide karnaa, plan karnaa, start honaa*). Mastering these 50 native verbs handles ~90% of all verbal situations.
> 2. **Nouns (~100 Non-Negotiable Anchors):** Hinglish speakers borrow hundreds of modern nouns directly from English (*office, pub, meeting, train, flight, bill, plan, problem, coffee*). Memorizing formal Hindi words for these (like *aspataal* or *railgaadi*) creates artificial, stiff speech. Instead, this list captures the 100 core concepts native speakers **instinctively keep in Hindi** (*baat, waqt, rasta, jagah, faisla, umeed, farq, paani, dhoop*).
> 3. **Adjectives (~50 High-Yield Descriptors):** English adjectives are common in Hinglish (*chill, weird, stressful*), but core physical states, sensory qualities, and visceral emotions are almost universally kept in Hindi (*garam/thandaa, aasaan/mushkil, mehengaa/sastaa, saaf/gandaa, zaroori, ajeeb*).
