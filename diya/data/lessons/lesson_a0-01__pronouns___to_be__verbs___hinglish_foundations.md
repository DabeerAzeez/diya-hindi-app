# 🌟Summary

Lesson 1 focuses on building the core foundation of spoken Hinglish by establishing the Subject-Verb agreement rules for the verb "To Be" (Am, Is, Are) and learning basic social greetings, locations, and state descriptions. It establishes the foundational Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) structure that aligns directly with Tamil sentence mechanics, making it effortless to construct natural, everyday sentences.


# 📘Lesson: Lesson 1 — Pronouns, "To Be" Verbs & Hinglish Foundations


## Key Grammar Formulas & Rules

* **Pronoun-Verb Pairing:**
$\text{Subject} + \text{Complement/Location} + \text{"To Be" Verb}$
* **Core Rule:** In present tense sentences, the verb "To Be" comes at the very end of the sentence and must agree with the subject pronoun.

## 📌 Core Pronouns & "To Be" Conjugation Table

| Pronoun | Hindi | Tamil Parallel | "To Be" Ending | Example (Hinglish) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I** | *Main* | *Naan* | **... hoon** | *Main Toronto me hoon.* |
| **You** *(Casual)* | *Tum* | *Nee* | **... ho** | *Tum ready ho?* |
| **You** *(Formal/Respect)* | *Aap* | *Neengal* | **... haim** | *Aap busy haim.* |
| **He / She / This** | *Ye / Vo* | *Ivar / Avar* | **... hai** | *Vo mera friend hai.* |
| **We / They** | *Hum / Vo* | *Naangal / Avargal* | **... haim** | *Hum ready haim.* |

* **1. The Core Rule: Nearby vs. Far Away**
• **`Ye`**** (यह / ये)** = **This / This person** (Close to you physically, or close in conversation).  
    ◦ **Tamil Equivalent:** **`Ivar`**** / ****`Idhu`**** / ****`Ivan`**** / ****`Ival`**
• **`Vo`**** (वह / वो)** = **That / That person** (Far from you, or not physically present).  
    ◦ **Tamil Equivalent:** **`Avar`**** / ****`Adhu`**** / ****`Avan`**** / ****`Aval`**

## Polyglot Anchors & Hinglish Mechanics

* **Tamil Alignment:** Sentences follow standard SOV order, matching Tamil's subject-location/complement-verb sequence (e.g., *Naan Toronto-la irukken* $\rightarrow$ *Main Toronto me hoon*).
* **Hinglish Integration:** Essential English nouns and adjectives (e.g., *busy*, *ready*, *friend*, *office*) slot directly into the complement position without needing complex translation.

## Real-World Applications & Catch-Up Scenarios

* **Greeting Friends:** Everyday casual check-ins with friends in Toronto or planning the October reunion with Shivani.
    * **Example:** `Aaj main busy hoon, lekin kal main free hoon. — आज मैं busy हूँ, लेकिन कल मैं free हूँ.`
<small>Aaj (Today) | main (I) | busy (busy) | hoon (am) | lekin (but) | kal (tomorrow) | main (I) | free (free) | hoon (am)</small>

# ❓Quiz


### Concept & Grammar Questions

<details><summary>**How does Hindi's word order differ from English, and how does it map to Tamil?**</summary>

* **Answer:** English uses Subject-Verb-Object (SVO), whereas Hindi uses Subject-Object-Verb (SOV). This aligns 1:1 with Tamil sentence structure (e.g., "I in Toronto am" / *Naan Toronto-la irukken*).
</details>

<details><summary>**Which "To Be" verb ending pairs with the pronoun ****`Main`**** vs ****`Tum`**** vs ****`Aap`****?**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Main` pairs with `hoon`, `Tum` pairs with `ho`, and `Aap` pairs with `haim`.
</details>


### Translation Questions

<details><summary>**Hello! How are you (formal)?**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Namaste! Aap kaise haim? — नमस्ते! आप कैसे हैं?`
<small>Namaste (Hello) | Aap (You - formal) | kaise (how - male) | haim (are)</small>
</details>

<details><summary>**I am in Toronto right now.**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Main abhi Toronto me hoon. — मैं अभी Toronto में हूँ.`
<small>Main (I) | abhi (right now) | Toronto (Toronto) | me (in) | hoon (am)</small>
</details>

<details><summary>**What's up? Everything is fine.**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Kya chal rahaa hai? Sab badhiyaa hai. — क्या चल रहा है? सब बढ़िया है.`
<small>Kya (What) | chal rahaa hai (is running/going on) | Sab (everything) | badhiyaa (fine/great) | hai (is)</small>
</details>

<details><summary>**He is my friend.**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Vo mera friend hai. — वो मेरा friend है.`
<small>Vo (He/That person) | mera (my - male) | friend (friend) | hai (is)</small>
</details>

<details><summary>**Today I am busy, but tomorrow I am free.**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Aaj main busy hoon, lekin kal main free hoon. — आज मैं busy हूँ, लेकिन कल मैं free हूँ.`
<small>Aaj (Today) | main (I) | busy (busy) | hoon (am) | lekin (but) | kal (tomorrow) | main (I) | free (free) | hoon (am)</small>
</details>

<details><summary>**Are you ready for the trip?**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Kya tum trip ke liye ready ho? — क्या तुम trip के लिए ready हो?`
<small>Kya (Question marker) | tum (you) | trip (trip) | ke liye (for) | ready (ready) | ho (are)</small>
</details>

<details><summary>**Main office me hoon.**</summary>

* **Answer:** I am in the office.
</details>

<details><summary>**Vaise, aap kahaan haim?**</summary>

* **Answer:** By the way, where are you?
</details>

<details><summary>**Hum Toronto me haim.**</summary>

* **Answer:** We are in Toronto.
</details>

<details><summary>**Vo Norwich me hai.**</summary>

* **Answer:** She is in Norwich.
</details>
