# Lesson A1-04.5: Postpositions & Oblique Case Consolidation


# 🌟Summary

Is lesson mein hum postpositions ke real impact ko master karenge: **Oblique Case**, aur daily conversation ka sabse essential structure: **"Chahiye"** (want / need).

Tamil grammar ki tarah jahan case markers root noun ko modify karte hain (jaise *vīṭṭil* ya *enakku vēṇḍum*), Hindi mein jab bhi koi postposition (*ko*, *me*, *par*, *se*, *ka/ki/ke*) aata hai, toh usse pehle aane wala pronoun ya masculine noun direct form se **oblique form** mein shift ho jaata hai.

$$\text{Subject} + \text{ko} + \text{Object} + \text{chahiye}$$

| **Pronoun (Direct)** | **Pronoun + Postposition (ko)** | **Oblique Meaning** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Main** (I) | **Mujhe** / *Mujhko* | To me (I need/want) |
| **Aap** (You - formal) | **Aapko** | To you |
| **Tum** (You - informal) | **Tumhe** / *Tumko* | To you |
| **Yeh** (This / He / She) | **Isko** / *Ise* | To this / him / her |
| **Woh** (That / He / She) | **Usko** / *Use* | To that / him / her |
| **Hum** (We) | **Hamein** / *Humko* | To us |


# 📘Lesson


## 📌 1. The Oblique Rule & Core Postpositions

Jab bhi kisi noun ya pronoun ke baad postposition lagta hai, use Oblique Case kehte hain.

* **Masculine Singular ****`aa`**** endings change to ****`e`****:**
    * *Kamra* (room) + *me* $\longrightarrow$ **Kamre me** (in the room)
    * *Rasta* (road) + *par* $\longrightarrow$ **Raste par** (on the road)
    * *Ladka* (boy) + *ko* $\longrightarrow$ **Ladke ko** (to the boy)
* **Feminine nouns & non-****`aa`**** masculine nouns do not change in singular:**
    * *Gadi* (car) + *me* $\longrightarrow$ **Gadi me**
    * *Office* + *me* $\longrightarrow$ **Office me**
$$\text{Noun (-aa)} + \text{Postposition} \longrightarrow \text{Noun (-e)} + \text{Postposition}$$


## 🎯 2. Expressing Needs & Desires: "Chahiye" Structure

Hindi mein "I want X" bolne ke liye direct subject use nahi hota; Tamil ke *enakku vendum* structure ki tarah Dative Subject (*Mujhe*) use hota hai.

* **Formula:**
    * $$\text{Person} + \text{ko} + \text{Item} + \text{chahiye}$$
* **Examples:**
    * Mujhe chai **chahiye**. (I want tea.)
    * Aapko kya **chahiye**? (What do you want / need?)
    * Dabeer ko thoda rest **chahiye**. (Dabeer needs some rest.)

## ⚡ 3. Wanting to Do an Action (Verb + Chahiye)

Jab aapko koi kaam karna chahiye (should do) ya karna hai, tab verb ki root ke saath `-na` (infinitive) laga kar `chahiye` use karte hain.

$$\text{Subject} + \text{ko} + \text{Verb (Infinitive: -na)} + \text{chahiye}$$

* Mujhe abhi nikal**na** **chahiye**. (I should leave now.)
* Hamein daily Hindi practice kar**na** **chahiye**. (We should practice Hindi daily.)
* Aapko commute time par start kar**na** **chahiye**. (You should start your commute on time.)

# ❓Quiz


### Concept & Grammar Questions

1. Explain what happens to the masculine noun ending in `aa` when a postposition is added.
    * **Answer:** The singular masculine `aa` ending changes to `e` (Direct: *kamra* $\rightarrow$ Oblique: *kamre me*).
1. Why is it incorrect to say *"Main chai chahiye"*?
    * **Answer:** *Chahiye* requires the dative subject marker (*ko*). The correct form is *"Mujhe chai chahiye"* (To me, tea is needed).
1. How is the Hindi *"Mujhe chahiye"* similar to Tamil grammar?
    * **Answer:** In Tamil, you use the dative case *enakku vēṇḍum* (to-me is-wanted) rather than the nominative *nān*; Hindi mirrors this exact structure with *Mujhe* (*Main + ko*) *chahiye*.
1. What form does a verb take when used with *chahiye* to mean "should do"?
    * **Answer:** The verb stays in its full infinitive form ending in `na` (e.g., *karna chahiye*, *dekhna chahiye*).
1. What is the oblique pronoun form of *Yeh* with *ko*?
    * **Answer:** *Isko* (or *Ise*).

### Translation Questions

1. **I need some water.**
    * **Answer:** `Mujhe thoda paani chahiye.`
    * Mujhe (To me) | thoda (some/a little) | paani (water) | chahiye (needed/wanted)
1. **What do you want? (formal)**
    * **Answer:** `Aapko kya chahiye?`
    * Aapko (To you) | kya (what) | chahiye (wanted)
1. **We should leave for the office now.**
    * **Answer:** `Hamein abhi office ke liye nikalna chahiye.`
    * Hamein (To us) | abhi (now) | office ke liye (for office) | nikalna chahiye (should leave)
1. **He wants a new acoustic guitar.**
    * **Answer:** `Usko ek naya acoustic guitar chahiye.`
    * Usko (To him) | ek (a/one) | naya (new) | acoustic guitar | chahiye (wanted)
1. **The boy is sitting in the room.**
    * **Answer:** `Ladka kamre me baitha hai.`
    * Ladka (Boy - direct subject) | kamre me (in the room - oblique) | baitha hai (is seated)
1. **You should speak slowly. (informal, m)**
    * **Answer:** `Tumhe aaram se bolna chahiye.`
    * Tumhe (To you) | aaram se (slowly/calmly) | bolna chahiye (should speak)
1. **Do you need help with this script? (formal)**
    * **Answer:** `Kya aapko is script me help chahiye?`
    * Kya (Question marker) | aapko (to you) | is script me (in this script - oblique) | help chahiye (need help)
1. **Mujhe thoda time chahiye.**
    * **Answer:** I need some time.
1. **Aapko daily exercise karna chahiye.**
    * **Answer:** You should exercise daily.
1. **Use yeh rasta maloom hai.**
    * **Answer:** He/She knows this route.
