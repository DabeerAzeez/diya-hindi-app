# Lesson A0-08: Simple Future Tense & Expressing Plans


# 🌟 Summary

Lesson 5 covers the **Future Tense** in Hindi, focusing on how to express **Life Plans**, **Upcoming Events**, and **Future Intentions** (*"will do", "will meet", "will go"*).

Since Hindi is an SOV language like Tamil, future tense sentences follow the structure: **Subject + [Object/Place/Time] + Future Verb Stem + Ending**.


### Key Takeaways

1. **Future Verb Endings (Male Speaker Perspective):**
    * **`Main`**** (I):** Stem + **`\bar{u}\dot{n}gaa`** (e.g., *Main call karūṅgaa* = I will call)
    * **`Tum`**** (You - casual):** Stem + **`oge`** (e.g., *Tum kyaa karoge?* = What will you do?)
    * **`Aap`**** (You - formal):** Stem + **`enge`** (e.g., *Aap kab aayenge?* = When will you come?)
    * **`Hum`**** (We):** Stem + **`enge`** (e.g., *Hum milenge* = We will meet)
    * **`Vo / Ye`**** (He/She/They):** Stem + **`egaa`** (Singular) / **`enge`** (Plural/Formal)
1. **Key Irregular Future Shifts:**
    * **To Go (****`jaana`****):** `jaa-` + ending $\rightarrow$ **`jaaūṅgaa`** (I will go)
    * **To Take (****`lena`****):** `le-` $\rightarrow$ **`lūṅgaa`** (I will take)
    * **To Give (****`dena`****):** `de-` $\rightarrow$ **`dūṅgaa`** (I will give)
    * **To Be/Happen (****`hona`****):** `ho-` $\rightarrow$ **`hogaa`** (It will happen / It will be)
1. **Impersonal Future Expressions (****`Lagegaa`****):**
    * Expressing time or cost taking place in the future uses **`Thoḍaa time lagegaa`** (*It will take some time*).
1. **Polyglot & Tamil Alignment:**
    * Hindi `Main karūṅgaa` maps directly to Tamil *Naan pannuvaen*.
    * Hindi `Hum milenge` maps to Tamil *Naam sandhippom*.
    * Hindi `Time lagegaa` maps to Tamil *Time aagum / aagum*.

# 📘 Lesson


## 1. Future Tense Conjugation Rules

To form the future tense in Hindi, take the **verb stem** (drop the `-naa` from the infinitive) and attach the subject-specific suffix. For a **male speaker/subject**, the endings are:

MATH: \text{Verb Stem} + \text{Future Suffix (Masculine)} \longrightarrow \text{Future Verb}

| **Pronoun** | **Suffix** | **Example Verb: karna (To do)** | **Translation** | **Tamil Equivalent** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Main** (I) | **`-\bar{u}\dot{n}gaa`** | `Main karūṅgaa` | I will do | *Naan pannuvaen* |
| **Tum** (You - casual) | **`-oge`** | `Tum karoge` | You will do | *Nee pannuva* |
| **Aap** (You - formal) | **`-enge`** | `Aap karenge` | You will do | *Neengal pannuveengal* |
| **Ye / Vo** (He/She/This/That) | **`-egaa`** | `Vo karegaa` | He will do | *Avar pannuvaar* |
| **Hum** (We) | **`-enge`** | `Hum karenge` | We will do | *Naangal pannuvom* |
| **Vo / Ye** (They) | **`-enge`** | `Vo karenge` | They will do | *Avargal pannuvaargal* |


## 2. Expressing Future Plans & Intentions

Here is how you apply future tense endings in everyday conversational Hinglish:

* **Main kal aapko call karūṅgaa.** — मैं कल आपको call करूँगा।
Main (I) | kal (tomorrow) | aapko (to you - formal) | call karūṅgaa (will call - male)
* **Weekend par kyaa karoge?** — Weekend पर क्या करोगे?
Weekend par (on weekend) | kyaa (what) | karoge (will do - casual male)
* **Hum jaldi milenge.** — हम जल्दी मिलेंगे।
Hum (We) | jaldi (soon) | milenge (will meet)
* **Main try karūṅgaa.** — मैं try करूँगा।
Main (I) | try karūṅgaa (will try - male)
* **Main 10 mins me pahuñchūṅgaa.** — मैं 10 mins में पहुँचूँगा।
Main (I) | 10 mins me (in 10 minutes) | pahuñchūṅgaa (will reach - male)

## 3. Impersonal Future: Time & Cost (`Lagegaa`)

When talking about how long something will take or cost in the future, Hindi uses the verb **`lagna`** (to take/feel) in its neutral future form **`lagegaa`**:

MATH: \text{Time / Detail} + \text{Lagegaa}

* **Thoḍaa time lagegaa.** — थोड़ा time लगेगा।
Thoḍaa (Some) | time (time) | lagegaa (will take)
* **Kitna time lagegaa?** — कितना time लगेगा?
    * Kitna (How much) | time (time) | lagegaa (will take)

## 4. Conjugation Comparison Table Across Core Verbs

Comparing common verbs for a male subject (`Main` / `Tum` / `Aap` / `Hum`):

| **Infinitive Verb** | **Main (I)  MD** | **Tum (You)  MD** | **Aap (You - formal)  MD** | **Hum (We)  MD** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Jaana** (To go) | `Main jaaūṅgaa` | `Tum jaaoge` | `Aap jaayenge` | `Hum jaayenge` |
| **Aana** (To come) | `Main aaūṅgaa` | `Tum aaaoge` | `Aap aayenge` | `Hum aayenge` |
| **Milna** (To meet) | `Main milūṅgaa` | `Tum miloge` | `Aap milenge` | `Hum milenge` |
| **Dekhna** (To see/watch) | `Main dekhūṅgaa` | `Tum dekhoge` | `Aap dekhenge` | `Hum dekhenge` |
| **Khaana** (To eat) | `Main khaaūṅgaa` | `Tum khaaoge` | `Aap khaayenge` | `Hum khaayenge` |


# ❓ Quiz


### Concept & Grammar Questions

1. **What is the future tense ending for ****`Main`**** (I) when spoken by a male speaker?**
    * **Answer:** **`\bar{u}\dot{n}gaa`** (e.g., *Main karūṅgaa*).
1. **How do the verb endings differ between ****`Tum`**** (casual) and ****`Aap`**** (formal) in the future tense?**
    * **Answer:** `Tum` takes **`oge`** (e.g., *Tum karoge*), while `Aap` takes **`enge`** (e.g., *Aap karenge*).
1. **How do you express "It will take some time" in Hindi? What verb is used?**
    * **Answer:** `Thoḍaa time lagegaa.` The verb stem used is from `lagna` $\rightarrow$ `lagegaa`.
