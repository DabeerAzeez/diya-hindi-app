# Lesson A1-08: Transitive Verbs & Ergative Case Marker (Ne)


# 🌟Summary

In this lesson, we conquer the single most defining architectural pillar of conversational Hindi: **The Ergative Case Marker (*Ne*)** and **Past Transitive Verb Agreement**.

Whenever an action is completed in the past using a **transitive verb** (a verb that takes a direct object, like *eating, seeing, drinking, making, sending, asking*), Hindi undergoes an elegant structural flip:

1. The subject takes the postposition **`ne`**.
1. Because the subject now has a postposition, it enters the oblique case and **relinquishes grammatical control of the verb**.
1. The verb agrees dynamically in gender and number with the **direct object** instead!
1. If the direct object is marked with **`ko`** (specific person or specific object), agreement is blocked, and the verb defaults to the neutral **masculine singular (`-aa`)**.

### 📐 Mathematical Formulas

$$\text{Subject} + \text{ne} + \text{Object} + \text{Verb}_{(\text{agrees with Object})}$$

$$\text{Subject} + \text{ne} + \text{Object} + \text{ko} + \text{Verb}_{(\text{default masculine singular: } -aa)}$$

$$\text{Maine} + \text{coffee (f)} + \text{pee (f)}$$

$$\text{Maine} + \text{Shivani (f)} + \text{ko} + \text{dekhaa (default -aa)}$$


### 🌐 Polyglot Linguistic Parallels

* **French Connection:** Think of the *passé composé* agreement with preceding direct objects (*La pomme que j'ai mangée* — the verb agrees with the apple, not the speaker!). In Hindi past tense, the verb *always* agrees with the object unless blocked by *ko*.
* **Tamil Connection:** In Tamil, the subject retains nominative agency, but passive/instrumental structures use *-āl* (e.g., *ennāl seyyappattadu*). Hindi's *ne* turns the active past into an ergative construction where the focus naturally balances between actor and outcome.

# 📘Lesson


## ⚡ 1. Intransitive vs. Transitive Verbs: When Does "Ne" Apply?

The rule is strictly binary and depends on the **nature of the verb**:

| Verb Type | Definition | Completed Past Rule | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Intransitive** | Verbs with **no direct object** (motion, state, bodily function) | **NO `ne`**. Verb agrees with the **subject**. | *Jaanaa* (to go), *Aanaa* (to come), *Baithnaa* (to sit), *Sonaa* (to sleep) |
| **Transitive** | Verbs that **act upon a direct object** | **REQUIRES `ne`**. Verb agrees with the **object**. | *Khaanaa* (to eat), *Peenaa* (to drink), *Dekhnaa* (to see), *Karnaa* (to do), *Sunnaa* (to hear) |


### Contrast:

* *Intransitive:* **Main gayaa.** (I went — agrees with male speaker *Main*).
* *Transitive:* **Maine pizza khaayaa.** (I ate pizza — *khaayaa* agrees with masculine noun *pizza*).

---


## 👥 2. Pronoun Transformations with "Ne"

When pronouns combine with `ne`, they merge into dedicated, high-frequency spoken forms:

| Base Pronoun | + Ne | Romanized Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Main** (I) | Main + ne | **Maine** | I (in past transitive) |
| **Hum** (We) | Hum + ne | **Humne** | We |
| **Tum** (You, casual) | Tum + ne | **Tumne** | You |
| **Aap** (You, formal) | Aap + ne | **Aapne** | You |
| **Ye** (This / He / She close) | Is + ne | **Isne** | He / She / This |
| **Vo** (That / He / She far) | Us + ne | **Usne** | He / She / That |
| **Ye** (These / They close) | In + hone | **Inhone** | They / Respected He/She |
| **Vo** (Those / They far) | Un + hone | **Unhone** | They / Respected He/She |
| **Kaun** (Who) | Kis + ne | **Kisne** | Who? (Who did it?) |

> **Pro-Tip:** In Hinglish conversation, you will use **Maine**, **Tumne**, **Aapne**, and **Usne** in nearly 90% of your storytelling anecdotes!

## 🎯 3. Dynamic Object Agreement (The Magic Rule)

Because the subject is locked behind `ne`, the verb looks to the **Object** for instructions:

```

[Subject + ne] + [OBJECT] + [VERB]

│         ▲

└─────────┘ (Verb matches Object!)

```


### 1. Masculine Singular Object $\to$ Verb ends in `-aa`

* *Maine ek email bhejaa.* (I sent an email — *email* is treated as masc).
* *Usne khaanaa banaayaa.* (He/She made food).

### 2. Feminine Singular Object $\to$ Verb ends in `-ii`

* *Maine chaay pee.* (I drank tea — *chaay* is fem).
* *Tumne guitar khareedii?* / *Aapne coffee pee?* (Did you buy a guitar? / Did you drink coffee?).

### 3. Masculine Plural Object $\to$ Verb ends in `-e`

* *Maine do gaane sune.* (I listened to two songs).
* *Usne saare documents bheje.* (He/She sent all the documents).

### 4. Feminine Plural Object $\to$ Verb ends in `-iin` (nasalized `-ī`)

* *Maine do films dekheen.* (I watched two movies).
* *Shivani ne puraanii photos bhejeen.* (Shivani sent old photos).

---


## 🛡️ 4. The "Ko" Neutralizer (The Blocking Rule)

What happens when you talk about a specific person (e.g., *"I saw Shivani"* or *"I called the doctor"*), where the person takes the object postposition `ko`?

* When an object takes **`ko`**, it ALSO goes into the oblique case!
* Now **both** the subject and the object are blocked by postpositions (`ne` and `ko`).
* With nobody left to tell the verb what to do, the verb defaults to the neutral **Masculine Singular (`-aa`)**:
$$\text{Maine} + \text{Shivani} + \text{ko} + \text{dekhaa} \quad (\text{NOT dekhii!})$$

$$\text{Usne} + \text{doctor} + \text{ko} + \text{call kiyaa}$$

$$\text{Maine} + \text{doston} + \text{ko} + \text{invite kiyaa}$$

## 🔄 5. Irregular Past Transitive Verbs

These four verbs are used constantly in spoken Hinglish and feature slight vowel contractions in the past:

| Infinitive | Root Meaning | Masc Sing (`-aa`) | Fem Sing (`-ii`) | Masc Plur (`-e`) | Fem Plur (`-iin`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Karnaa** | To do | **kiyaa** | **kii** | **kiye** | **keen** |
| **Lenaa** | To take | **liyaa** | **lii** | **liye** | **leen** |
| **Denaa** | To give | **diyaa** | **dii** | **diye** | **deen** |
| **Peenaa** | To drink | **peeyaa** | **pee** | **peeye** | **peen** |

* Examples:
* *"Maine kaam kiyaa."* (I did the work).
* *"Aapne chaabiyaan leen?"* (Did you take the keys?).
* *"Usne mujhe address diyaa."* (He/She gave me the address).

---


## 🚫 6. The 4 Rebel Verbs (Transitive WITHOUT "Ne")

A handful of verbs in Hindi logically look like transitive verbs, but historically and grammatically **NEVER take `ne`**. The subject stays in the direct case and the verb agrees with the **subject**:

1. **Bolnaa** (To speak / produce words):
    * *Main bolaa.* (I spoke — male).
    * *Shivani bolii.* (Shivani spoke — female).
    * *(Avoid "Maine bolaa" in careful grammar; use "Maine kahaa" if you want to use ne).*
1. **Laanaa** (To bring):
    * Contraction of *le + aanaa* (literally "take and come" — motion verb!).
    * *Main pizza laayaa.* (I brought pizza).
    * *Shivani gift laayī.* (Shivani brought a gift).
1. **Bhoolnaa** (To forget):
    * *Main apnaa password bhool gayaa.* (I forgot my password).
1. **Samajhnaa** (To understand):
    * *Main samajh gayaa!* (I understood!).

# ❓Quiz


### Concept & Grammar Questions

<details><summary>**Why does the sentence *"Maine coffee pee"* end in *-ee* even when spoken by a male speaker?**</summary>

* **Answer:** Because the subject *Main* takes the ergative marker *ne* (*Maine*), which relinquishes subject agreement. The verb must agree with the direct object *coffee*, which is grammatically feminine.
</details>

<details><summary>**In the sentence *"Maine Shivani ko dekhaa"*, why is the verb *dekhaa* (masculine) instead of *dekhii* (feminine)?**</summary>

* **Answer:** The object *Shivani* is followed by the postposition *ko*. When both subject (with *ne*) and object (with *ko*) have postpositions, agreement is blocked, and the verb defaults to the neutral masculine singular ending (*-aa*).
</details>

<details><summary>**Which of the following verbs NEVER take the ergative marker *ne* in the completed past: *dekhnaa, jaanaa, laanaa, karna, bolnaa*?**</summary>

* **Answer:** *Jaanaa* (intransitive), *laanaa* (exception / compound of *le + aanaa*), and *bolnaa* (rebel exception) never take *ne*.
</details>

### Translation Questions

<details><summary>**I drank coffee this morning. (male speaker)**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Maine aaj subah coffee pee.` — मैंने आज सुबह कॉफ़ी पी।
* [Maine (I + ne) + aaj (today) + subah (morning) + coffee (coffee, f) + pee (drank, f)]
</details>

<details><summary>**Did you listen to my new song? (casual / to Shivani)**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Tumne meraa nayaa gaanaa sunaa?` — तुमने मेरा नया गाना सुना?
* [Tumne (you + ne) + meraa nayaa gaanaa (my new song, m) + sunaa (heard/listened, m)]
</details>

<details><summary>**Shivani told me about Norwich.**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Shivani ne mujhe Norwich ke baare mein bataayaa.` — शिवानी ने मुझे नॉर्विच के बारे में बताया।
* [Shivani ne (Shivani + ne) + mujhe (to me) + Norwich ke baare mein (about Norwich) + bataayaa (told, default m)]
</details>

<details><summary>**Who did this work?**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Yeh kaam kisne kiyaa?` — यह काम किसने किया?
* [Yeh kaam (this work, m) + kisne (who + ne) + kiyaa (did, m)]
</details>

<details><summary>**I sent two emails before the meeting.**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Maine meeting se pehle do emails bheje.` — मैंने मीटिंग से पहले दो ईमेल भेजे।
* [Maine (I + ne) + meeting se pehle (before meeting) + do emails (two emails, m.pl) + bheje (sent, m.pl)]
</details>

<details><summary>**I brought pizza for everyone. (Remember the rebel verb rule!)**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Main sabke liye pizza laayaa.` — मैं सबके लिए पिज़्ज़ा लाया।
* [Main (I, direct) + sabke liye (for everyone) + pizza (pizza) + laayaa (brought, agrees with male subject, NO ne)]
</details>

<details><summary>**We made dinner together.**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Humne saath mein dinner banaayaa.` — हमने साथ में डिनर बनाया।
* [Humne (we + ne) + saath mein (together) + dinner (dinner, m) + banaayaa (made, m)]
</details>

<details><summary>**Did you see Shivani's message? (formal / respectful)**</summary>

* **Answer:** `Aapne Shivani kaa message dekhaa?` — आपने शिवानी का मैसेज देखा?
* [Aapne (you + ne) + Shivani kaa message (Shivani's message, m) + dekhaa (saw, m)]
</details>
