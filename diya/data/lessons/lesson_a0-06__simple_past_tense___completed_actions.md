# Lesson A0-06: Simple Past Tense & Completed Actions


# 🌟 Summary

Lesson 4 covers the **Simple Past Tense** in Hindi, focusing on **Past States** (*"was/were"*), **Intransitive Actions** (*"went", "came", "happened"*), and **Transitive Completed Actions** (*"ate", "drank", "saw", "did"*).

Since Hindi is an SOV language like Tamil, simple past sentences follow the structure: **Subject + [Object/Place/Detail] + Past Verb**.


### Key Takeaways

1. **Past "To Be" (Was / Were):**
    * **`Thaa`** — Masculine Singular (e.g., *Main ready thaa* = I was ready)
    * **`The`** — Masculine Plural / Formal Respect (e.g., *Aap kahaan the?* = Where were you?)
    * **`Thee`** — Feminine Singular
1. **Intransitive / Motion Actions (Subject-Agreeing Past Verbs):**
    * **Went:** `Gayaa`
    * **Came:** `Aayaa`
    * **Happened:** `Huaa`
    * **Understood:** `Samajh gayaa`
1. **Transitive Actions (****`ne`**** Ergative Shift & Object Agreement):**
    * Transitive verbs (verbs acting on an object like *eat*, *drink*, *see*, *do*, *buy*) require adding the postposition **`ne`** to the subject (`Maine`, `Tumne`, `Aapne`, `Usne`).
    * The verb agrees with the **object** (or stays neutral masculine `aa` for Hinglish nouns).
1. **Polyglot & Tamil Alignment:**
    * Hindi `thaa` maps directly to Tamil *irundhen* / *irundhadhu*.
    * Hindi `gayaa` maps to Tamil *poanaen* (*poayittaen*).
    * Hindi `samajh gayaa` maps to Tamil *purinjiduchu*.

# 📘 Lesson


## 1. Past States: Was & Were (`Thaa` / `The`)

When describing where you were, how a day was, or a state in the past, use `thaa` for masculine singular subjects (I / he / it) and `the` for formal address (*Aap*) or plural subjects.

MATH: \text{Subject} + \text{Place / Adjective} + \text{Thaa / The}

* **Main busy thaa.** — मैं busy था।
    * Main (I) | busy (busy) | thaa (was - male)
* **Kal bahut busy din thaa.** — कल बहुत busy दिन था।
    * Kal (Yesterday) | bahut (very) | busy (busy) | din (day) | thaa (was)
* **Aap kahaan the?** — आप कहाँ थे?
    * Aap (You - formal) | kahaan (where) | the (were - formal)

## 2. Completed Intransitive Actions (Motion & State Verbs)

For basic movement and common intransitive verbs in the past, the verb agrees directly with the subject. For a male speaker, attach masculine endings (`-aa` / `-yaa`):

MATH: \text{Subject} + \text{Destination / Detail} + \text{Past Verb}

| **English Action** | **Present Form** | **Past Form (Male Speaker)** | **Tamil Equivalent** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **To Go** | `jaana` | **`Gayaa`** | *Poanaen* |
| **To Come** | `aana` | **`Aayaa`** | *Vandhaen* |
| **To Happen** | `hona` | **`Huaa`** | *Aachu / Nadandhadhu* |
| **To Understand** | `samjhna` | **`Samajh gayaa`** | *Purinjiduchu* |

* **Main kaam par gayaa.** — मैं काम पर गया।
    * Main (I) | kaam (work) | par (at/to) | gayaa (went - male)
* **Main samajh gayaa.** — मैं समझ गया।
    * Main (I) | samajh gayaa (understood / completely got it - male)
* **Main nahi samjha.** — मैं नहीं समझा।
    * Main (I) | nahi (not) | samjha (understood - male)
* **Kyaa huaa?** — क्या हुआ?
    * Kyaa (What) | huaa (happened)

## 3. Intransitive Past Tense

Because `jaana` is irregular in the past tense (`jaataa` → `gayaa`),  but more importantly since it’s an intransitive verb, the past participle agrees with the *subject. *Note below we’re assuming the subjects are all male, but if they were female they would all be “-i” (singular/informal) / “-in” (formal/plural) endings.

| **Pronoun** | **Present Habitual (Goes / Go)** | **Simple Past (Went)** | **Tamil Equivalent (Past)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Main** (I) | `Main jaataa hoon` | **`Main gayaa`** | *Naan poanaen* |
| **Tum** (You - casual) | `Tum jaate ho` | **`Tum gaye`** | *Nee poana* |
| **Aap** (You - formal) | `Aap jaate haim` | **`Aap gaye`** | *Neengal poaneengal* |
| **Ye / Vo** (He / This / That) | `Ye/Vo jaataa hai` | **`Ye/Vo gayaa`** | *Ivar/Avar poanaar* |
| **Hum** (We) | `Hum jaate haim` | **`Hum gaye`** | *Naangal poanoam* |
| **Vo / Ye** (They) | `Vo/Ye jaate haim` | **`Vo/Ye gaye`** | *Avargal poanaargal* |


## 4. Transitive Past Tense: `ne` Ergative Shift & Object Agreement

Verbs that take a direct object (e.g., *eat*, *drink*, *see/watch*, *do*, *buy*, *order*) trigger a special rule in Hindi past tense: the **Ergative Postposition ****`-ne`**.


### Rule A: Pronoun + `ne` Shift

MATH: \text{Subject Pronoun} + \text{ne} \longrightarrow \text{Ergative Subject Form}

| **Pronoun** | **+ ne** | **Ergative Past Form** | **Tamil Equivalent** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Main** (I) | `Main + ne` | **`Maine`** | *Naan... (transitive past)* |
| **Tum** (You - casual) | `Tum + ne` | **`Tumne`** | *Nee...* |
| **Aap** (You - formal) | `Aap + ne` | **`Aapne`** | *Neengal...* |
| **Ye** (He / She / This) | `Is + ne` | **`Isne`** | *Ivar / Idhu...* |
| **Vo** (He / She / That) | `Us + ne` | **`Usne`** | *Avar / Adhu...* |
| **Hum** (We) | `Hum + ne` | **`Humne`** | *Naangal...* |
| **Vo / Ye** (They) | `Unhone / Inhone` | **`Unhone`** | *Avargal...* |

Now for `dekhnaa` — note that the past participle is the same throughout. That’s because this is a *transitive *verb, so the ending depends on the *object*, not the subject. Here we’re assuming a male object [that you are `dekhnaa`-ing].

| **Pronoun** | **Present Habitual (Sees / See)** | **Simple Past (Saw)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Main** (I) | Main dekhtaa hoon | Main ne dekhaa |
| **Tum** (You - casual) | Tum dekhte ho | Tum ne dekhaa |
| **Aap** (You - formal) | Aap dekhte hain | Aap ne dekhaa |
| **Ye / Vo** (He / This / That) | Ye / Vo dekhtaa hai | Is ne / Us ne dekhaa |
| **Hum** (We) | Hum dekhte hain | Hum ne dekhaa |
| **Vo / Ye** (They / These / Those) | Vo / Ye dekhte hain | Unhon ne / Inhon ne dekhaa |


### Rule B: Object Agreement

Once you attach `-ne`, the subject loses control of the verb! The verb now agrees with the **object**, or defaults to a neutral masculine form (`-aa`) when using English Hinglish nouns:

MATH: \text{Ergative Subject (Maine / Tumne / Usne)} + \text{Object} + \text{Past Verb}

* **Maine coffee peeyi.** — मैंने coffee पी।
    * Maine (By me) | coffee (coffee - feminine) | peeyi (drank - feminine ending)
* **Tumne kyaa kiyaa?** — तुमने क्या किया?
    * Tumne (You - casual) | kyaa (what) | kiyaa (did - neutral male ending)
* **Aapne show dekha?** — आपने show देखा?
    * Aapne (You - formal) | show (show - male) | dekha (watched - male ending)
* **Usne cocktail order kiyaa.** — उसने cocktail order किया।
    * Usne (He/She) | cocktail (cocktail) | order kiyaa (ordered - neutral male ending)

## Verb Types

Hindi past tense me main rule simple hai:
• **Consonant-ending roots:**
Jab verb ka root consonant par khatam hota hai, tab hum sirf **-aa** add karte hain (*-yaa* nahi!).
    ◦ Root: **soch-** (from *sochnaa*) $\rightarrow$ `soch + aa = socha` [sochaa — सोचा]
    ◦ Root: **dekh-** (from *dekhnaa*) $\rightarrow$ `dekh + aa = dekha` [dekhaa — देखा]
    ◦ Root: **bol-** (from *bolnaa*) $\rightarrow$ `bol + aa = bola` [bolaa — बोला]


• **Vowel-ending roots:**
Jab verb root vowel par khatam hota hai, tab smooth pronunciation ke liye **-yaa** lagta hai.
    ◦ Root: **khaa-** (from *khaanaa*) $\rightarrow$ `khaa + yaa = khaayaa` [khaayaa — खाया]
    ◦ Root: **banaa-** (from *banaanaa*) $\rightarrow$ `banaa + yaa = banaayaa` [banaayaa — बनाया]
    ◦ Root: **laa-** (from *laanaa*) $\rightarrow$ `laa + yaa = laayaa` [laayaa — लाया]




# ❓ Quiz


### Concept & Grammar Questions

1. **Explain when you use ****`thaa`**** versus ****`the`**** in spoken Hindi.**
    * **Answer:** `Thaa` is used for masculine singular subjects (I / he / it). `The` is used for formal respect (*Aap*) or masculine plural subjects (we / they).
1. **What is the difference between intransitive verbs (like ****`jaana`****) and transitive verbs (like ****`khaana`****) in the past tense?**
    * **Answer:** Intransitive verbs agree directly with the subject (e.g., `Main gayaa`). Transitive verbs require attaching `ne` to the subject (e.g., `Maine`), and the verb agrees with the *object* rather than the speaker.
1. **What happens to the pronouns ****`Main`****, ****`Tum`****, ****`Aap`****, and ****`Vo`**** when you use them in the transitive past tense?**
    * **Answer:** They shift to their ergative forms: `Maine`, `Tumne`, `Aapne`, and `Usne`.
1. **How do you say "What happened?" versus "What did you do?" in Hindi?**
    * **Answer:** "What happened?" is `Kyaa huaa?` (intransitive). "What did you do?" is `Tumne kyaa kiyaa?` (transitive with `ne`).
